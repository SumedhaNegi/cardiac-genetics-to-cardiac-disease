# Methods summary: cardiac transcription-factor binding footprints

Published cardiac chromatin-occupancy datasets were used to construct genomic
annotations associated with HOPX and five established cardiac regulators:
GATA4, MEF2A, NKX2-5, TBX5 and SRF. The thesis describes data from mouse heart
tissue and HL-1 cardiomyocytes, including foetal and adult cardiac profiles from
Akerberg et al.

Gene sets were compared using pairwise Jaccard similarity and set intersections.
Functional enrichment was assessed using Gene Ontology and KEGG resources.
SNP-level annotations were then used in partitioned heritability analyses with
SBayesRC to estimate per-SNP heritability enrichment across cardiac and
cardiovascular traits. Developmental-stage analyses distinguished adult-specific,
foetal-specific and shared regulatory annotations.

The thesis reports extensive overlap among cardiac regulator-associated gene
sets. TBX5, SRF and NKX2-5 showed the greatest pairwise similarity; 184 genes
were shared across all six annotations. Gene Ontology and KEGG analyses
highlighted cardiac contraction, transcription-factor binding, dilated
cardiomyopathy, MAPK and Hippo signalling. Heritability enrichment was above
one across diverse traits, but no TF annotation showed a markedly high
comparative enrichment. Adult-specific regulatory regions showed consistently
higher mean cardiac-trait enrichment than foetal-specific regions.

These are annotation-level enrichment results. They do not establish that every
gene or regulatory interval is causal, nor do they identify causal variants or
demonstrate molecular mediation.
